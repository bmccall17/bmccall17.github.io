Implementation Rescue & Feature Audit

Reducing friction by removing 40% of the product roadmap.

Situation

Philanthrosphere®, a legacy fundraising platform, was suffering from feature bloat. The engineering team was overwhelmed by a roadmap of "nice-to-have" features, while data entry compliance from end-users (non-profit admins) was declining due to a confusing UI.

Obstacles

  • Sunk Cost Fallacy: Stakeholders were attached to features "we always planned to build."
  • User Confusion: The core "Donor Loop" (entering a contact, logging a touchpoint) was buried under 5+ clicks.
  • Dev Velocity: The team was spending 60% of time maintenance on unused features.

Actions

  • Usage Audit: Instrumented the app to reveal that 40% of deployed features had <5% monthly active usage.
  • Ruthless Subtraction: Deprecated low-value features to focus entirely on the "Donor Loop."
  • Simplified Workflow: Redesigned the data entry flow to be a single-page modal, reducing clicks by 70%.

Results

  • 30% Reduction in Dev Cycles: Freeing up engineering to focus on stability and core value.
  • Adoption Lift: Data entry compliance increased significantly as friction was removed.
  • Clarity: Stakeholders regained confidence in the product direction.

What I'd Repeat

"Code is a liability, not an asset." I would start every implementation rescue with a subtraction audit before proposing new features.
