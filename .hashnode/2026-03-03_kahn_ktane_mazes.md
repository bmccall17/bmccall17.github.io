# The Kahn moment

Exactly 25 years ago today, legendary Bayern Munich goalkeeper Oliver Kahn, trailing Hansa Rostock in the final minutes, sprinted up for a corner kick. In a moment of pure instinct and desperation, he punched the ball into the opponent's net with both fists. He was sent off, but it earned him legendary status.

Sometimes risk taking leads to really, really bad ideas. These ideas aren't worth harvesting or iterating on. They are simply raw data points for what *not* to do. Kahn proved that goalkeepers cannot, in fact, handle the ball in the opponent's penalty area.

# The Maze

But there's another side to failure. While some risks are just bad ideas, other risks and "bad ideas" are actually there to teach us exactly where the path is or is *not*.

This is one of the core lessons from my favorite module in the game *Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes*: the **[Maze](https://ktane.fandom.com/wiki/Maze)**. 

In the Maze module, you are navigating an invisible layout. Hitting a wall isn't just a failure. It is essential sensory feedback, the wall appears on the module if you attempt to move through it. The "bad idea" of walking into a wall is exactly what informs you where the boundaries are. It reveals the shape of the terrain. The risk teaches you where the path is *not*, which directly illuminates where the path *is*.

# Distillation

Some failures are just bad ideas. Punching the ball in the opponent's box. Don't emulate them.
Other failures are echolocation they can literally help us find our way. Bumping into the invisible walls of a maze to learn its shape.
Know which one you are experiencing.
