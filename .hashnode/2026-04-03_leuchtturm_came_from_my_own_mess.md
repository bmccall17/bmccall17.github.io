## I did not arrive at this idea from a whiteboard.

I arrived here from a pile of half-working flows, weird little wins, broken deploys, analytics tabs, emulator sessions, agent prompts, and the very real feeling of shipping something on tuesday and wondering by wednesday if it was healthy, fragile, being used, or quietly dying.

The last stretch of my life has changed how i see software.

For a long time i was adjacent to building. I designed experiences. I facilitated transformation. I helped people adopt tools. I translated between humans and systems. I knew how product friction felt in the room. I knew how onboarding either opened a door or made people shut down. I knew how the wrong level of complexity could kill momentum. I knew all that from the people side.

Then AI cracked something open for me.

Suddenly i could build more of my own ideas without waiting on a full engineering team. I could move from concept to working prototype fast enough to stay in conversation with the idea. That changed everything. I started shipping.

I built experiments.
I built landing pages.
I built flows.
I built agents.
I built strange little products that were real enough to break in public.

I worked on agent828 and the context lake behind it. I pushed on sidexside. I kept shaping TarotTALKS. I started looking at things like repOptics and other ideas through the lens of actual product behavior instead of pure concept. I found myself in WSL, in Vercel, in Supabase, in PostHog, in Stripe sandbox, in Android emulators, in dashboards, in logs, in config files, in the guts of things.

That has been one of the biggest shifts in my life.

I am no longer just someone with ideas about product.
I am someone who can get a version live.

## Shipping is only the first threshold.

Once i could ship, a new hunger showed up immediately.

I wanted to know what was happening.
I wanted to know where users were getting stuck.
I wanted to know which flows were healthy.
I wanted to know which features were getting ignored.
I wanted to know when something changed on the surface and quietly broke downstream.
I wanted to know if a deploy helped or hurt.
I wanted to know whether i had a product, a toy, a ghost town, or a time bomb.

I did what a lot of us do. I reached for the tools that already existed.

Some of them were powerful. Some of them were impressive. Some of them were clearly built by serious people for serious teams.

And over and over i felt the same mismatch.

I do not need a control room built for a hundred engineers to understand whether three critical flows in my product are alive.
I do not need a giant internal platform to keep track of a handful of services, user events, experiments, and integrations.
I do not need a massive taxonomy and a year of setup to answer the basic human questions every small builder asks after launch.

```
what changed
what broke
what is being used
what is being ignored
where are people dropping
what needs my attention today
```

That gap is the story.

## AI has created a new class of builder.

Some of us are solo.
Some of us are pairs.
Some of us are tiny teams.
Some of us come from design, operations, facilitation, product, teaching, community, art, or strategy.
Some of us are technical. Some of us are becoming technical in public.

We can ship faster than ever.
We can glue together real products faster than ever.
We can test markets faster than ever.
We can get to first users faster than ever.

And then we hit the wall.

The tools for understanding and managing what we built still assume a larger organization, deeper infrastructure maturity, more headcount, more ceremony, and more time than many of us have.

That is where leuchtturm lives for me.

## The product

Leuchtturm is a lightweight operating layer for builders who already use great tools but still cannot clearly see the health, risk, ownership, and next-best action across their stack.

Small teams do not need a port authority. They need a lighthouse.

The product connects the tools a modern small team already uses — GitHub, Vercel, Sentry, Supabase, Snyk, Cloudflare, Railway — and turns them into a clear operating picture. Not "another dashboard." a synthesis engine plus an action layer.

The first ten minutes should create relief. Connect one repo and one deploy platform. Leuchtturm auto-generates a system map. It shows you what is healthy, what is risky, what is drifting. It computes a release confidence score. It tells you what to fix first.

The MVP wins if people say:
- *"It showed me something important immediately"*
- *"It made me feel calmer"*
- *"It told me what to fix first"*
- *"I do not want to go back to guessing"*

## The personal version is even simpler.

It comes from being in the middle of my own learning curve.
It comes from moving from idea person to builder.
It comes from wanting my products to have better optics.
It comes from wanting observability that fits the size and reality of the work.
It comes from wanting quick wins during setup instead of a long pilgrimage.
It comes from wanting to see my world clearly without pretending i am running a Fortune 500 engineering org.

I think there is a real market here because the market itself has changed.

Solo devs are real builders now.
New devs are real builders now.
Tiny teams are real product studios now.
People using AI as a force multiplier are shipping things that deserve care, feedback loops, and operational visibility.

They do not need less seriousness.
They need the right-sized seriousness.

## Distillation

I am building more than i ever have in my life. I am learning fast. I am messing things up. I am getting better. I am seeing the same pain again and again.

I can ship now.

I want to keep what i ship alive. I want to understand it. I want to improve it. I want the tools around me to match the actual scale and texture of my work.

Leuchtturm comes from that desire.

It comes from lived need. It comes from my own recent evolution. It comes from the strange and beautiful pressure of becoming the kind of builder who can no longer hide behind the sentence "someone should make this."

Now i am one of the people making it.

And i know i am far from the only one.

* * *

Leuchtturm is early. The pitch doc is written. The figma is taking shape. The connectors are ahead of us. But the thing that is real right now is the problem — and the problem is deeply, personally mine.

That is usually where the best stuff starts.
