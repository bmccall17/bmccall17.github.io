17 Tribes, One Paved Road

Platform Engineering Engineering Organization & Culture

With 500+ engineers across 17 autonomous tribes, Sportradar faces a challenge that most organizations never reach: how do you maintain speed, quality, and reliability when every team has the freedom to choose their own tooling?

The Sportify Model

Adopted in mid-2019 and fully actualized by 2022, Sportradar's engineering organization is modeled after Spotify's scaled agile methodology — internally called "Sportify."

The structure follows the familiar hierarchy: Tribes → Squads → Chapters → Guilds. Each squad is cross-functional and self-sufficient. DevOps specialists are embedded within squads, not centralized. Platform teams serve as a central function, bridged to squads through an Engineering Enablement Group.

The Core Principle: Autonomy

Squads and tribes are free to choose their own technology and tooling. This autonomy drives innovation and speed — but it also creates a coordination challenge when services from different tribes need to work together during a live sporting event.

The "Paved Roads" Approach

Sportradar's platform engineering philosophy rests on four pillars — and a principle borrowed from infrastructure design: build highways, not walls.

1. Team Restructuring for Self-Sufficiency

Each squad has the expertise to build features end-to-end. No dependency on a central team for basic operations. The goal is autonomy without isolation.

2. Paved Roads (Golden Paths)

A shared common stack for observability, SDLC, CI/CD, and frameworks. Teams can deviate — but the paved road is always the fastest path.

"If you divert off the paved road, you can still get to those outcomes, but it's going to take you a little bit more time."

— Scott Dunbar, SVP Engineering Enablement

3. Core Metrics

Every squad measures against three axes: availability, reliability, and performance. All measured against SLOs. A 99.9% availability target across 200+ business services.

4. Managed Services Catalog

An internal developer portal with project bootstrapping templates, opinionated service stacks, scorecards for all applications, and centralized hosting and networking.

"An essential element of platform engineering is listening to what your customers and community needs and wants, and delivering on those."

— Scott Dunbar, SVP Engineering Enablement

Observability as a Quality Gate

Sportradar uses New Relic as the standardized "pane of glass" across all teams. Technical SLIs bubble up into business services for comparison against SLOs. A custom observability app was built using synthetics data. The result: a 15% increase in release frequency through observability practices.

"The creation of a system where we can debug unknown unknowns."

— Scott Dunbar, SVP Engineering Enablement

The Remaining Challenge

Sportradar has solved observability. They've built paved roads. They have scorecards and SLOs. But when an alert fires during a live Premier League match, the context needed to resolve it — service ownership, recent deployments, dependency maps, on-call rotations — still lives across six different tools.

The monitoring tells you what's broken. It doesn't tell you who owns it, what changed, or who to call.
