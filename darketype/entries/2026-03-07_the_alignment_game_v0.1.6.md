# the problem
i had a success site that spoke in generics — "Technical CSM/TAM" — and a cover letter + resume that spoke in specifics for Port.io. the site needed to tell the same story the application tells.

# the learning
the best portfolio site for a job application isn't generic. it's a mirror of the cover letter. every section should make the hiring manager feel like "oh, this person already gets us." the site should feel like a conversation with someone who's already doing the work, not a brochure.

# the mess
- i had to decide what to keep general vs. what to Port-ify. went too specific in some places at first (mentioning Austin location as a bullet) and pulled it back.
- the human-in-the-loop trust model language was the hardest to land. it's a real thing i do daily but it reads like buzzword soup if you're not careful. kept iterating until it felt like practitioner truth, not marketing copy.
- realized my old "key achievements" were underselling — the 30% dev cycle reduction at Philanthrosphere is real but pales next to 65% revenue growth and 43% retention. lesson: lead with the strongest numbers.
